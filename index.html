Strategic Collaboration: Quantum-Safe OTA Security for Software-Defined Electric Vehicles

Following our recent meeting, we would like to share an updated overview of the proposed partnership. We recommend structuring this as a technology-integrated, revenue-sharing partnership in which QNu Labs earns more as Svashasan OS gains commercial adoption. Rather than treating QNu as a security vendor that sells us a product once, we see QNu as the long-term cryptographic security partner of Svashasan OS.

Svashasan provides the operating system and automotive integration layer; QNu provides the quantum-safe security technology; together, we can offer OEMs a unified, secure software-update and vehicle-software protection solution.

We believe this has a credible technical foundation. QNu publicly positions its portfolio around post-quantum cryptography (PQC), quantum random number generation (QRNG), key management and its QShield security platform. It also explicitly identifies automotive OTA updates and connected-vehicle security as target use cases.

The commercial objective is for QNu to earn a slightly larger share of the joint security opportunity, without transferring ownership of Svashasan OS, its kernel architecture, or its overall licensing business.

1. The Strategic Proposition

Svashasan Labs — the mobility software layer

Owns the Svashasan OS architecture, vehicle-side integration, OTA workflow orchestration, update lifecycle, compatibility layer and OEM integration.

QNu Labs — the cryptographic security layer

Provides licensed quantum-safe cryptographic components, key-management integration and other agreed security capabilities through its applicable products and SDKs, subject to technical validation and agreed licensing.

Joint offering — security for the vehicle software lifecycle

An integrated OTA security solution that verifies update authenticity, protects update delivery, manages cryptographic trust and helps prevent unauthorised software from being installed.

We see the partnership as an integrated capability for OEMs, not merely an encryption add-on. It should be designed to support automotive cybersecurity engineering and software-update management processes, including applicable requirements under ISO/SAE 21434 and UNECE Regulations R155 and R156. Integration of cryptography alone does not establish regulatory compliance.

2. What We Build Together

We propose a jointly marketed product, working name:

Svashasan Secure OTA — secured by QNu Labs

The goal is to secure the software-update lifecycle for EVs running Svashasan OS, while keeping Svashasan's underlying operating system and update orchestration under Svashasan's control.

QNu's automotive portfolio publicly identifies three particularly relevant capabilities: its QuSafe HSM development platform, PQC-enabled TLS communication, and quantum-safe protection for charging systems. It also describes HODOS as its PQC library. These are suitable starting points for technical evaluation, not a guarantee that every component is already production-ready for the target vehicle hardware.

  • Check hardware compatibility and version policy.

  • Validate post-install health.
  • Recover through an approved rollback or recovery mechanism.

The security requirements that matter most

The implementation should not equate encryption with secure OTA. Multiple controls need to operate together.

  • Authenticity and integrity: Each approved update must be signed, and the vehicle must reject packages whose signature or manifest fails verification.
  • Key protection: Protect signing keys, restrict signing access and define revocation and rotation procedures. The cloud signing service and vehicle-side trust anchors must have clearly separated responsibilities.
  • Anti-rollback protection: Prevent unauthorised installation of an older, vulnerable software version.
  • Secure transport: Protect the update-delivery connection using an agreed cryptographic configuration, including PQC where supported and appropriate.
  • Recovery and auditability: Handle interrupted updates safely, record security-relevant events and support investigation of failed or suspicious releases.
  • Cryptographic agility: Design interfaces so that approved algorithms can be changed as standards and OEM requirements evolve.

For the first implementation, we propose evaluating NIST's standardised ML-DSA digital-signature standard (FIPS 204) for update authenticity, and ML-KEM (FIPS 203) for key establishment where required. We would ask QNu to confirm the precise algorithms, versions, performance and validation status available in its proposed components before the design is committed.

Important architecture decision: QKD should not be a prerequisite for secure OTA over ordinary vehicle cellular connectivity. We suggest starting with suitable PQC, digital signatures, sound key management and vehicle-side verification. QRNG or dedicated security hardware should be introduced only where the risk assessment, hardware design and measured performance justify them.

3. The Commercial Model: QNu Earns More When Svashasan Succeeds

This is the most important part of the proposal. We would prefer to avoid a conventional arrangement in which Svashasan pays a large upfront licence fee and QNu's revenue remains largely independent of whether a joint OEM deployment succeeds.

Instead, we propose structuring the partnership around one recurring, jointly sold security product.

Recommended revenue model

Proposed recurring security revenue split

QNu Labs          60%
Svashasan Labs   40%

Negotiation proposal, not an existing agreement or a representation of either company's costs or margins.

QNu would earn 60% of net collected revenue from the joint security subscription. Svashasan would earn 40% for OS integration, product orchestration and OEM delivery.

We would open negotiations at this 60:40 split in QNu's favour, subject to the scope of QNu's contribution. This gives QNu the larger share of the security-specific opportunity, while allowing Svashasan to build a recurring revenue stream without surrendering the economics of its core OS.

Define the revenue base carefully

The split should apply to the Net Security Revenue of the joint product, not to Svashasan Labs' entire revenue.

We propose defining it in the contract as cash collected from the jointly sold security subscription, less only specifically agreed deductions:

  • GST and other applicable indirect taxes collected for remittance.
  • Refunds, credits and customer discounts expressly agreed by both parties.
  • Third-party pass-through charges for the joint product that are documented and approved in advance.

Neither party should reduce the revenue base by unapproved internal salaries, overheads or discretionary expenses.

Svashasan's independent OS licence, autonomy software, perception modules, navigation and vehicle-control revenue remain outside the split. QNu would not obtain a royalty over those unrelated products simply because its security component is embedded in Svashasan OS.

How the model works at different scales

We propose a per-vehicle annual subscription, or another OEM-approved recurring metric. The actual price must be negotiated after performance testing and customer discovery.

The model gives QNu three commercially meaningful advantages.

First, QNu receives the larger recurring share as the number of covered vehicles grows. Second, successful integration gives it an established route into OEM accounts reached through Svashasan. Third, as the product expands to more EV platforms, QNu can potentially sell additional, separately scoped security capabilities through the same relationship.

That makes QNu's success dependent on actual customer adoption and renewal of the joint product, rather than only on the initial technology sale.

4. A Larger Overall Benefit for QNu, Without Giving Away Svashasan OS

The 60:40 security revenue split is the economic foundation. The partnership becomes considerably more attractive to QNu when we add four further benefits.

1. A repeatable route to the automotive market

Svashasan integrates QNu's security capabilities into an EV operating system designed for OEM adoption. With the appropriate customer permissions and technical proof, QNu can use the integration as a reference for future OEM and Tier-1 discussions.

2. Recurring royalties instead of one-time licensing revenue

QNu earns from the active vehicle base or covered software licences each year. New vehicle programmes and renewals expand QNu's revenue without requiring it to acquire every customer independently.

3. An automotive integration and validation partner

Svashasan contributes its OS, simulation environment, vehicle integration work and agreed test results. QNu gains a route to evaluate its security components on selected automotive compute platforms and ECUs, reducing the effort needed to explore additional vehicle programmes.

4. Expansion into adjacent security products

Once OTA security is validated, the partners can jointly pursue ECU-to-ECU communication, secure vehicle identity, EV charging authentication and additional connected-vehicle security services. Each expansion should have its own scope, pricing and revenue agreement.

Why this is commercially attractive to QNu: QNu receives the larger recurring share of the joint product while gaining product-level integration into Svashasan's future automotive deployments. QNu's upside increases with Svashasan's distribution success, and Svashasan OS remains Svashasan's own.

We are not offering QNu access to all Svashasan customers, unrestricted use of proprietary code, or automatic rights to every future automotive product. Instead, we propose specific, measurable commercial benefits.

The provisions that protect the long-term relationship

Intellectual property: Svashasan retains ownership of its OS kernel, system architecture, OTA orchestration, integration code and pre-existing IP. QNu retains ownership of its cryptographic technology, libraries, key-management technology and pre-existing IP. Any jointly developed inventions must have a written ownership and licensing arrangement before development begins.

Exclusivity: We propose offering QNu preferred-partner status for the agreed quantum-safe OTA security module. We do not propose blanket exclusivity across Svashasan OS or all automotive cybersecurity. Any narrowly defined exclusivity should be conditional on technical delivery, support commitments and measurable commercial milestones.

Technology portability: Svashasan should use a defined security abstraction layer between its OTA system and QNu's implementation. This allows cryptographic components to be upgraded or replaced without rewriting the OS. Any replacement must preserve agreed security properties and undergo appropriate validation.

Support and liability: Define response times, vulnerability notifications, patches, supported configurations, product maintenance and escalation responsibilities. Allocate liability according to fault, contractual responsibility and applicable law. Automotive safety, cybersecurity and regulatory responsibilities cannot simply be transferred to the technology partner.

Audit and payment: Quarterly revenue statements, clear vehicle or licence counts, audit rights limited to the joint product, and payment within an agreed period after customer collections. Both parties should have visibility into the commercial metrics that determine their share.

6. Execute in Stages: From Technical Proof to OEM Revenue

The timeline below is a planning estimate, not a commitment. Automotive hardware access, OEM requirements and testing could extend it.

Stage 1 — Technical and commercial alignment

Weeks 1–2

Agree on the OTA use case, target hardware, cryptographic interfaces, revenue definition, responsibilities and pilot success criteria. Confirm which QNu products and licences apply.

Stage 2 — Proof of concept

Indicatively 6–8 weeks after access to the required SDKs and hardware

Integrate QNu's agreed security components into the Svashasan OTA workflow. Start in simulation, then test on representative development hardware where available.

Stage 3 — Security validation and OEM pilot

Timing subject to hardware and customer readiness

Test signature rejection, tamper detection, downgrade prevention, key rotation, recovery after interrupted updates, cryptographic latency, processor and memory overhead, and failure handling. Submit results to the OEM with a documented threat model and limitations.

Stage 4 — Production deployment and recurring revenue

Production integration would begin only after OEM acceptance, required validation and commercial approval. Per-vehicle or per-licence billing would then be activated, field performance monitored, and the solution expanded to additional vehicle programmes.

Define acceptance before writing the integration

At a minimum, both companies should agree in advance on these measurable test outcomes:

7. Proposed Next Steps with QNu Labs

Since an NDA is already in place, the next step should be a technical evaluation agreement followed by a commercial pilot MoU, rather than another generic confidentiality agreement.

We suggest leading with the joint market opportunity, then moving to proposed terms.

1. Confirm the technology

We would ask QNu to identify the exact security components it can license, the available SDKs, hardware dependencies, technical support and the boundaries of its existing product IP.

2. Propose a low-risk pilot

We propose an OEM-funded or capped-cost pilot. No unlimited engineering commitment, production hardware purchase or minimum-volume guarantee should be required before a credible customer deployment exists.

3. Offer the 60:40 recurring security revenue split

QNu receives the larger share. In return, we would request integration support, access to the necessary development components, jointly approved technical validation, and a commercially workable licence for deployment through Svashasan OS.

4. Establish a joint OEM pipeline

Svashasan can lead discussions about its operating system and vehicle integration; QNu can participate in security architecture reviews and technical demonstrations. Customer ownership, account coordination and rights to reuse reference material must be agreed in writing.

5. Agree on an expansion mechanism

Once the initial OTA security deployment achieves its agreed milestones, the partners can negotiate additional ECU security, charging security and connected-vehicle products under separate statements of work.

8. The Immediate Deal Proposal

Proposed Svashasan Labs × QNu Labs Strategic Partnership

Svashasan Labs proposes a strategic technology and commercial partnership with QNu Labs to integrate QNu's agreed quantum-safe cryptographic technologies into Svashasan OS, beginning with secure over-the-air software updates for electric vehicles.

Svashasan Labs will own the operating system, OTA orchestration, vehicle integration and OEM delivery, while QNu Labs will supply and support the agreed cryptographic security components and associated capabilities. The parties will jointly validate the solution against agreed hardware, cybersecurity and performance requirements before progressing to an OEM pilot.

Commercially, we propose a recurring revenue-sharing model under which QNu Labs receives 60% and Svashasan Labs receives 40% of net collected revenue attributable to the jointly offered security subscription. The model is designed so that QNu's commercial returns grow with the number of vehicles and customer programmes adopting Svashasan Secure OTA. Svashasan's independent OS and autonomy revenues will remain outside the revenue-sharing arrangement.

The partnership will preserve each company's background IP, establish clear technical support and liability boundaries, and provide a pathway for jointly pursuing additional automotive security opportunities. Pilot funding, technology licensing, deployment rights and commercial milestones will be documented in a definitive agreement.

Bottom Line

The strongest arrangement is not to position QNu Labs as a low-margin supplier to Svashasan. Instead, we propose giving QNu a recurring, 60% share of the specific security revenue it helps create, a pathway into OEM deployments, and opportunities to expand its technology footprint as Svashasan OS scales. In exchange, Svashasan would secure the licensing, engineering support and deployment rights it needs, without compromising ownership of its OS, kernel architecture or broader autonomy business.

The decisive next milestone is a joint technical workshop that ends with three signed-off outputs: the exact QNu technology to integrate, the pilot acceptance criteria, and the proposed 60:40 commercial framework.
